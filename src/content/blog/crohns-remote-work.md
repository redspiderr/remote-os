# Crohn's & Remote Work: My Story

I was diagnosed with Crohn's disease at twenty-three. I was also two years into a promising career at a tech company in San Francisco. The collision of those two facts nearly ended my professional life before it started.

Crohn's is an inflammatory bowel disease. The symptoms are not polite: abdominal pain, urgent and frequent bathroom trips, crushing fatigue, and malnutrition. Flares are unpredictable. A good week can turn into a nightmare overnight. There is no cure, only management.

## The Office Was a Hostile Environment

The open-plan office felt designed to punish invisible illness. Every desk was in sight of a manager. Bathroom trips were tracked, intentionally or not. When I needed to leave a meeting suddenly, the raised eyebrows followed me out the door.

I used up my sick days in the first quarter of every year. I lied about why I was late. I pretended I was fine because explaining Crohn's to a busy engineering manager felt like oversharing. The stress of hiding the disease made the disease worse. The loop was vicious.

## Remote Work Changed Everything

When the company went fully remote, the first thing I noticed was the bathroom door was my own. I could manage symptoms without an audience. I could schedule doctor appointments without asking permission. I could lie down for twenty minutes after a bad morning and make up the time in the evening.

But remote work alone was not enough. Early in the pandemic, synchronous culture followed us home. Zoom fatigue replaced commute fatigue. Calendar Tetris replaced hallway anxiety. I was still performing wellness for a camera.

## Async Gave Me Dignity

The real transformation came when our team shifted to async communication. Video standups replaced live meetings. Written updates replaced status calls. I could contribute on my own schedule, on my good days and my bad days, without explaining why my camera was off or why I missed a standup.

Async work is an accessibility tool disguised as a productivity hack. For people with chronic illness, invisible disabilities, caregiving responsibilities, or non-traditional sleep schedules, it removes the performative layer of office life. Your output matters more than your presence. Your ideas matter more than your stamina.

## What I Built

That experience is why I joined MEDINA OS. REMOTE OS is not just a standup tool; it is a tool I wish I had when I was terrified that my body would cost me my career.

The features we prioritize—transcription for people who cannot always speak clearly, flexible deadlines for people whose energy is unpredictable, and a culture that values updates over attendance—are built from lived experience.

## A Note to Managers

If you lead a remote team, assume someone on your team is managing an invisible condition. You do not need to know the details. You do need to build systems that do not punish people for biology.

Async-first culture is the most inclusive policy you can adopt. It costs nothing. It benefits everyone. And for some of your people, it is the difference between a thriving career and a medical resignation.

## Conclusion

Remote work saved my career. Async work saved my dignity. I am not an edge case; I am a preview of the future workforce. Chronic illness, neurodivergence, caregiving, and mental health challenges are not rare. They are normal human variation.

The companies that build for variation will attract the best talent. The ones that insist on synchronous, presence-based culture will filter out the very people who could transform their teams.

Build for humans. The rest follows.
